PROLOGUE

Behind the Frozen Waterfall

Rocky Valley · the third morning

Behind a curtain of shining ice, Chloe and Leo found a cave. A round, sleepy wombat with a snow-white coat blocked the way, chewing a carrot. "Nobody enters my cave," he grumbled, "unless they can share fairly. Half for you, half for me — no more, no less. Can you even do that?"

"Halves are easy!" said Leo. "That's just splitting into two equal parts!"

The Wombat raised one bushy eyebrow. "We'll see about that, small human. We'll see."

EPISODE 1Fractions: ½ and ¼

Halves and Quarters

The mouth of the cave
Previously: A fair-minded Snow Wombat guards a cave and demands everything be shared fairly…

"A half (½)," said the Wombat, "is 1 of 2 equal parts. A quarter (¼) is 1 of 4 equal parts. Cut my carrot wrong and you get nothing!" A glowing chocolate bar appeared, ready to be shared.

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Fair Fractions
Solve all five to satisfy the fussy Wombat.
  1. A chocolate bar has 4 equal squares. What is half of it?
  2. What is a quarter (¼) of 4 squares?
  3. You cut a pizza into 2 equal pieces. Each piece is called a ___ ?
  4. Half of 10 marbles is how many?
  5. A quarter of 8 lollies is how many?
Solutions: 1) 2 squares   2) 1 square   3) half   4) 5   5) 2

The Wombat sniffed, impressed despite himself. "Hmph. Not bad. But sharing between TWO is easy. Come deeper — where things must be shared between many."

EPISODE 2Sharing Equally (Division)

The Sharing Stones

A cavern of glowing crystals
Previously: The Wombat is impressed — now the cave opens into a cavern where treasure must be split many ways…

"Dividing," said the Wombat, "is sharing equally into groups. 12 crystals shared between 3 friends — put them out one-by-one until they're gone: 4 each!" Piles of glowing crystals waited to be shared correctly.

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Sharing Stones
Share each pile fairly with none left over.
  1. Share 12 crystals equally between 3 friends. How many each?
  2. Share 10 lollies equally between 2 friends. How many each?
  3. Share 15 stickers equally among 5 kids. How many each?
  4. 8 apples shared between 4 people — how many each?
  5. 20 sweets shared between 4 friends — how many each?
Solutions: 1) 4   2) 5   3) 3   4) 2   5) 5

Every pile split perfectly, glowing bright. "Fairly done," the Wombat admitted. "Now — can you MEASURE fairly too? Follow me to the Hall of Rulers."

EPISODE 3Length (cm & m)

The Hall of Rulers

A long tunnel lined with giant measuring sticks
Previously: Crystals shared, the Wombat leads on to a hall where everything must be measured…

"Length," said the Wombat, "is how long something is. We measure small things in centimetres (cm) and big things in metres (m). 100 cm make 1 metre!" Icicles of every size hung from the ceiling, each needing to be measured.

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Measuring Length
  1. Which would you measure in metres, not centimetres: a pencil, or a ski slope?
  2. An icicle is 10 cm long. Another is 5 cm. How much longer is the first?
  3. How many centimetres make 1 metre?
  4. Leo's ski is 90 cm and Chloe's is 100 cm. Whose is longer?
  5. A ribbon is 20 cm. You cut it in half. How long is each piece?
Solutions: 1) a ski slope   2) 5 cm   3) 100   4) Chloe's   5) 10 cm

At the tunnel's end stood a great stone door with ten locks. "The Door of Fair Shares," rumbled the Wombat. "Prove all you've learned, and my deepest treasure is yours to share."

🔒 BIG CHALLENGE 1

The Door of Fair Shares

A ten-lock stone door · mixes Episodes 1–3
Previously: At the tunnel's end, a ten-lock door tests fractions, sharing, and length together…
🔒 THE TEN-LOCK DOOR — solve all ten!
  1. What is half of 8?
  2. What is a quarter of 12?
  3. Share 18 crystals between 3 friends — how many each?
  4. Share 16 lollies between 4 friends — how many each?
  5. How many cm make 1 metre?
  6. An icicle is 14 cm; you break off 6 cm. How long is what's left?
  7. Half of 20 sweets is how many?
  8. A cake is cut into 4 equal slices. What fraction is 1 slice?
  9. Which is longer: 1 metre or 50 cm?
  10. Share 21 stickers among 3 kids — how many each?

The great door split down the middle — into two equal halves! — and swung open. Beyond lay a chamber of gleaming scales and jars. "The last tests," said the Wombat. "Weight… and water."

EPISODE 4Mass (grams & kilograms)

The Weighing Chamber

A room full of golden balance scales
Previously: The Door of Fair Shares opened into a chamber of scales and jars…

"Mass," said the Wombat, "is how heavy something is. Light things we weigh in grams (g); heavy things in kilograms (kg). 1000 grams make 1 kilogram!" A golden scale waited to be balanced.

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Balancing the Scales
  1. Which is heavier: a feather or a snowball?
  2. Would you weigh a person in grams or kilograms?
  3. One side of a scale has 3 kg. The other has 5 kg. Which side is heavier?
  4. A bag holds 2 kg of snow. Another holds 2 kg of feathers. Which is heavier? (Careful!)
  5. A snowball weighs 100 g. Two snowballs weigh how much?
Solutions: 1) a snowball   2) kilograms   3) the 5 kg side   4) they weigh the same — 2 kg is 2 kg!   5) 200 g

The scale balanced with a soft chime. "One test remains," said the Wombat, leading them to a row of jugs and bottles. "Can you measure water fairly?"

EPISODE 5Capacity (litres)

The Jugs of the Melt-Water Spring

A crystal spring deep in the cave
Previously: The scales balanced; only the water puzzles remain before the treasure…

"Capacity," said the Wombat, "is how much a container can hold. We measure it in litres (L). A big bottle of juice is about 1 litre; a bathtub holds a lot more!"

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Measuring Water
  1. Which holds more: a teaspoon or a bucket?
  2. A jug holds 2 litres. You pour out 1 litre. How much is left?
  3. You fill a 1-litre bottle 3 times. How many litres in total?
  4. Would you measure a swimming pool in litres or teaspoons?
  5. A jug holds 4 litres, shared equally into 2 cups. How many litres per cup?
Solutions: 1) a bucket   2) 1 litre   3) 3 litres   4) litres   5) 2 litres

The Wombat's eyes twinkled. "You have measured, shared, and split everything fairly. You are ready for the Vault of Fair Shares — my greatest test."

🏆 GRAND FINALE

The Vault of Fair Shares

The heart of the cave · mixes all of Book Three
Previously: Everything led here — a golden vault with fifteen locks of fractions, sharing, and measuring…
🏆 THE FIFTEEN-LOCK VAULT — the final challenge!
Everything from Book Three, easy → hard. Take 30 minutes!
  1. What is half of 6?
  2. What is a quarter of 8?
  3. Share 12 sweets between 4 friends — how many each?
  4. Share 20 crystals between 5 friends — how many each?
  5. How many cm make 1 metre?
  6. How many grams make 1 kilogram?
  7. A ribbon is 30 cm; cut in half — how long is each piece?
  8. A jug holds 6 litres; pour out 2 — how much is left?
  9. Which is heavier: 3 kg or 300 g?
  10. A cake in 4 equal slices — what fraction is 2 slices?
  11. Half of 14 is how many?
  12. Share 24 stickers among 3 kids — how many each?
  13. Which holds more: a cup or a bathtub?
  14. A quarter of 20 is how many?
  15. Two 1-litre bottles and one 1-litre bottle — how many litres in all?
Vault code: 1) 3   2) 2   3) 3   4) 4   5) 100   6) 1000   7) 15 cm   8) 4 litres   9) 3 kg   10) 2 quarters = one half   11) 7   12) 8   13) a bathtub   14) 5   15) 3 litres
Final rank: 13–15 = 🏆 Master of Fair Shares! · 9–12 = 🥈 Brilliant! · 5–8 = 🥉 True explorer · under 5 = share the cave again, you'll get it!

The vault opened, and inside — not gold, but a feast: enough warm food to share with every hungry animal on the mountain. The Wombat wiped a happy tear. "The real treasure was always sharing," he sniffled. "Take this map. Tomorrow you climb the Tower of Numbers." And he handed them a scroll marked with a tall, spiralling tower.
