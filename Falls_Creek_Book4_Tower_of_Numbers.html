PROLOGUE

The Tower That Grows

The high ridge · the fourth morning

On the highest ridge stood a tower of ice bricks, so tall its top vanished into the clouds. A shimmering dragon of frost coiled around it. "I am the keeper of Numbers," it rumbled. "This tower grows one floor higher each time you understand a number truly. Reach the top, and a great prize is yours. But muddle your tens and ones, and the tower crumbles!"

Chloe cracked her knuckles. "We're ready. Let's climb."

EPISODE 1Place Value: Tens & Ones

Tens and Ones

The ground floor of the tower
Previously: A tower that grows with every number understood; the Snow Dragon dares them to climb…

"Every number is built from place values," said the Dragon. "In 34, the 3 means 3 tens (30) and the 4 means 4 ones. Tens and ones stacked together — just like the bricks of my tower!"

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Tens and Ones
  1. In the number 25, how many tens are there?
  2. In the number 47, how many ones are there?
  3. What number is made of 3 tens and 6 ones?
  4. What number is 5 tens and 0 ones?
  5. The number 62 — is the 6 worth 6 or 60?
Solutions: 1) 2 tens   2) 7 ones   3) 36   4) 50   5) 60

The floor beneath them glowed and a new floor built itself above — clink, clink, clink — bricks stacking into the sky. "Higher!" laughed the Dragon. "Now let us reach the hundreds!"

EPISODE 2Place Value: Hundreds

Into the Hundreds

The second floor
Previously: Tens and ones mastered, the tower grows — next stop, the hundreds…

"Beyond tens come hundreds," boomed the Dragon. "In 342, the 3 means 3 hundreds (300), the 4 means 4 tens (40), the 2 means 2 ones. Three hundred and forty-two!"

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Hundreds, Tens, Ones
  1. In 253, how many hundreds are there?
  2. What number is 1 hundred, 2 tens and 5 ones?
  3. In the number 408, how many tens are there?
  4. Which is bigger: 100 or 99?
  5. Write "two hundred and thirty" as a number.
Solutions: 1) 2 hundreds   2) 125   3) 0 tens   4) 100   5) 230

The tower shot up another floor, higher than the tallest gum tree. "Now," said the Dragon, eyes gleaming, "can you put numbers in ORDER? Chaos is not allowed in my tower!"

EPISODE 3Comparing & Ordering Numbers

The Hall of Order

A floor of floating number-tiles
Previously: Into the hundreds — now the Dragon demands numbers be put in perfect order…

"To compare numbers," said the Dragon, "look at the biggest place value first. More hundreds wins; if equal, more tens wins. We write > for 'greater than' and < for 'less than'." Number-tiles floated in the air, waiting to be sorted.

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Compare and Order
  1. Which is bigger: 45 or 54?
  2. Put in order, smallest first: 30, 13, 21.
  3. Fill the gap with > or <:   76 ___ 67
  4. Which is smallest: 100, 99, 110?
  5. Put in order, largest first: 8, 80, 18.
Solutions: 1) 54   2) 13, 21, 30   3) 76 > 67   4) 99   5) 80, 18, 8

The tiles snapped into tidy rows and the tower surged upward. High above, a landing appeared with a great door — sealed with ten glowing number-locks. "The Landing of Ten," said the Dragon. "Pass it, and half the tower is climbed."

EPISODE 4Rounding to the Nearest 10

The Rounding Winds

A windy balcony near the top
Previously: Half the tower climbed; now the Dragon teaches the magic of rounding…

"Rounding," said the Dragon, "means finding the nearest tidy ten. Look at the ones digit: if it's 5 or more, round UP; if it's 4 or less, round DOWN. So 28 rounds to 30; 23 rounds to 20." The wind carried numbers past, each needing to be rounded.

🔓 DECODE THIS! — Round to the Nearest 10
  1. Round 27 to the nearest 10.
  2. Round 42 to the nearest 10.
  3. Round 15 to the nearest 10. (5 rounds up!)
  4. Round 61 to the nearest 10.
  5. About how many is 38, rounded to the nearest 10?
Solutions: 1) 30   2) 40   3) 20   4) 60   5) 40

The winds calmed. Near the top, the tower opened into a room full of curious solid shapes — cubes, balls, and pointed pyramids of ice. "The Chamber of Solid Shapes," said the Dragon. "Flat shapes are for babies. Up here, shapes are REAL — they have faces you can touch!"

The Chamber of Solid Shapes

Near the summit of the tower
Previously: The rounding winds calmed; the tower opens into a chamber of 3D shapes and a mysterious chart…

"A cube has 6 flat square faces, like a dice. A sphere is a ball — no flat faces at all. A cylinder is like a can; a pyramid comes to a point." The Dragon also unrolled a chart it had made, counting how many skiers used each lift.

  3. Look at the chart: which lift had the MOST skiers?

The Dragon reared up proudly. "You have climbed nearly to the sky. Only the Crown of the Tower remains — my greatest test of all."
