## Text 1 — Narrative 故事类（推断加强）

**The New Girl**

Amara stood at the classroom door, holding her schoolbag straps so tightly that her knuckles turned white. Twenty-five faces turned to look at her.

"Class, this is Amara," said Mrs Chen. "She has just moved here from Perth. Who would like to be her buddy this week?"

For a moment, nobody moved. Amara stared at her shoes.

Then a hand shot up at the back of the room. "Me! I'll do it!" called a girl with red hair and a gap-toothed grin. "I'm Poppy. I moved here last year, so I know exactly how it feels."

At lunchtime, Poppy showed Amara the library, the canteen and the best handball court. "The trick," Poppy explained, "is to bring your own ball. Then everyone wants to play with you."

By home time, Amara's schoolbag straps hung loosely from her shoulders, and she was smiling. Perth felt very far away — but for the first time, that didn't seem like such a bad thing.

**Q3.** Why did Poppy volunteer to be Amara's buddy?

**Q4.** What is Poppy's "trick" about handball?

**Q5.** At the end, Amara's bag straps "hung loosely from her shoulders". This detail shows that Amara —

**Q6.** What is the main message of this story?

## Text 2 — Poetry 诗歌

**My Umbrella**

My umbrella is a mushroom
that blooms when clouds turn grey,
a little roof that follows me
and keeps the rain away.

The raindrops play their drum song —
*tap-tap*, *tappity-tap* —
while I stay dry as toast beneath
my mushroom's friendly cap.

But when the sun peeks out again
and paints the puddles gold,
I shake my sleeping mushroom dry
and fold away its fold.

**Q7.** In this poem, the umbrella is compared to —

**Q8.** Which word rhymes with **grey** in the first verse (stanza)?

**Q9.** "The raindrops play their drum song" means —

**Q10.** "I stay dry as toast" — the poet says this to show —

- D) she is hungry

**Q11.** What "paints the puddles gold"?

**Q12.** When does the poet fold the umbrella away?

## Text 3 — Information Report（图表混合，真题常见）

**Emperor Penguins**

Emperor penguins are the tallest and heaviest of all penguins. They live in Antarctica, the coldest place on Earth, where winter temperatures can drop below −60°C.

*Surviving the cold*
Emperor penguins have four layers of feathers and a thick layer of fat called blubber. When it is very cold, hundreds of penguins stand close together in a group called a huddle. They take turns standing on the outside, so everyone gets a turn in the warm middle.

*Raising a chick*
The mother penguin lays one egg and then travels up to 80 kilometres to the sea to find food. The father balances the egg on his feet, under a warm flap of skin, for about two months. During this time he eats nothing at all! When the mother returns, she feeds the new chick, and the hungry father finally goes to sea to eat.

## Text 4 — Persuasive（对立观点结构）

**Should Homework Be Banned?**

Dear Editor,

Last week your newspaper asked readers: should homework be banned in primary schools? I am nine years old, and I say NO — but let me explain, because my answer might surprise you.

I do not love homework. Nobody does! But a little bit of homework teaches us something important: how to organise our own time. When I finish my reading before dinner, I feel proud of myself.

Homework also shows our parents what we are learning. My dad says he would never know what I do at school if he didn't see my spelling words on the fridge.

However, I do think there can be too much homework. After a long day at school, children also need time to play, exercise and rest. Ten to twenty minutes a night is plenty for primary school.

So please, teachers — keep the homework, but keep it short!

Yours sincerely,
Ella Watson

**Q19.** What question is Ella answering in her letter?

- C) Should children watch less TV?
- D) Should spelling tests be harder?

**Q20.** What is Ella's opinion?

**Q21.** According to Ella, homework teaches children —
