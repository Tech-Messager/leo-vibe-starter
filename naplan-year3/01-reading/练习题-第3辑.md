## Text 1 — Narrative（多角色+情感转折）

**The Cricket Bat**

Nasir's grandfather's cricket bat lived on top of the wardrobe, wrapped in an old tea towel. Nobody was allowed to touch it.

"It played in Karachi in 1968," Dad always said, as if the bat had done the playing all by itself.

On the morning of the school cricket carnival, Nasir stood on a chair and lifted it down. The willow was pale and smooth, worn dark where his grandfather's hands had gripped it thousands of times. It was heavier than his own bat, and somehow warmer.

He told himself he would only hold it. Then he told himself he would only take it downstairs. By the time he reached the school gate, the bat was in his bag.

Nasir scored 34 runs that day — more than he had ever scored. But on the very last ball, there was a crack like a branch breaking. A thin split ran up the side of the bat, from the edge towards the handle.

Nasir's stomach turned to ice. He wrapped the bat in his jumper and carried it home like a wounded bird.

That night he put it on the kitchen table and waited for the storm.

Dad unwrapped it slowly. He ran his thumb along the split for a long, long time. Then, to Nasir's astonishment, he laughed — a short, surprised laugh, with something wet in his eyes.

"Thirty-four runs," Dad said quietly. "Do you know, my father would rather this bat cracked in a game than sat on a wardrobe for another forty years." He looked up. "But next time, Nasir, you ask me. That is the deal."

They mended the split together with glue and twine, and the bat went back on top of the wardrobe. It still lives there now. But every carnival day, Dad lifts it down himself.

- C) Nasir stopped playing cricket.

## Text 2 — Information Report（含数据表+图注）

**Bushfires and Banksias**

Bushfires can look like nothing but destruction. Yet some Australian plants have been living with fire for millions of years — and a few of them actually need it.

*Fire-loving seeds*
The banksia is one of these plants. Its woody cone holds seeds shut tight inside little "mouths". Only the heat of a bushfire makes the mouths open, dropping the seeds onto ground that has just been cleared of competing plants and enriched by ash. Within weeks, tiny banksia seedlings appear.

*Bouncing back*
Other plants survive in different ways:

| Plant | How it survives fire |
|-------|---------------------|
| Banksia | Cones open in heat and release seeds |
| Eucalypt | Buds hidden under thick bark regrow shoots |
| Grass tree | Growing point protected deep in the trunk |
| Wattle | Seeds wait in the soil for years until fire passes |

*Too much of a good thing*
However, scientists warn that fires which come **too often** do not give young plants enough time to grow up and make new seeds. If a forest burns every three or four years instead of every twenty, some species can disappear from the area completely.

*Caption under photo:* A banksia cone after fire, its seed "mouths" open wide.

## Text 3 — Procedure（含警示与推理）

**Growing a Bean in a Jar**

*You will need:*
- a clean glass jar
- a paper towel
- 2 or 3 dried beans
- water
- a sunny windowsill

*What to do:*
1. Fold the paper towel and push it around the inside of the jar, so it lines the glass.
2. Pour in a little water — just enough to make the paper towel damp. **Do not fill the jar.** Beans sitting in too much water will rot instead of sprouting.
3. Slide the beans between the paper towel and the glass, where you can see them.
4. Stand the jar on a sunny windowsill.
5. Check every day. Add a spoonful of water if the towel feels dry.

*What you should see:*
After 3 to 5 days a small white root will push downwards. A day or two later a pale shoot will grow upwards, and the first leaves will unfold and turn green in the light.

*Think about it:* Why does the root always grow downwards, even if you turn the jar around?

## Text 4 — 双文本比较（真题高阶题型）

**Text 4A — Letter to the school newsletter**

Dear Families,

From next term, our school canteen will no longer sell soft drinks. Instead we will offer water, plain milk and fresh juice.

We know some children will be disappointed. However, one can of soft drink can contain up to ten teaspoons of sugar. Dentists tell us that sugary drinks are the biggest cause of tooth decay in Australian children.

We would rather have a canteen that helps our students stay healthy than one that simply sells what is popular.

Yours sincerely,
Mrs Halim, Principal

**Text 4B — Letter from a student**

Dear Mrs Halim,

I am in Year 3 and I read your letter. I agree that too much sugar is bad for our teeth, and I think water is a great choice on hot days.

But I do not think banning soft drinks completely is fair. Some children only buy one on Fridays as a treat. Could we have "Fizzy Friday" once a term instead? That way we would still learn that soft drink is a *sometimes* food, not an *everyday* food.

Yours sincerely,
Yasmin Chowdhury

**Q21.** According to Mrs Halim, what is the biggest cause of tooth decay in Australian children?
- A) not brushing teeth — B) sugary drinks — C) fresh juice — D) plain milk

**Q22.** What do BOTH writers agree about?
- A) Soft drinks should be banned completely.
- B) Too much sugar is bad for children's teeth.
- C) The canteen should close.
- D) Water tastes better than soft drink.

**Q23.** How is Yasmin's opinion DIFFERENT from Mrs Halim's?
- A) Yasmin thinks sugar is healthy.
- B) Yasmin wants soft drinks sold every day.
- C) Yasmin thinks an occasional treat is fairer than a complete ban.
- D) Yasmin does not want any drinks sold at all.

**Q24.** Yasmin uses the words *sometimes* food and *everyday* food to —
- A) show she understands sugar can be part of a balanced diet
- B) argue that soft drinks are healthy
- C) explain the canteen opening hours
- D) list the drinks she likes best

### 本辑难点总结（给家长）
第3辑的四个高阶题型，做对说明达到 Exceeding 水平：
1. **递进心理描写题**（Q2）——看懂"一步步说服自己"的写法
2. **复杂情感题**（Q6）——人物可以同时有两种情绪
3. **主旨题的"两层结构"**（Q14）——正确选项必须涵盖全文两面，不能只对一半
4. **双文本比较**（Q22–23）——找相同点和不同点，NAPLAN 后半段常出现

如果只错这四类，其他全对，说明阅读已经很强，考前只需保持每周 2 篇的阅读量即可。
