# Paper 1 — Writing（40分钟）

> **Today you are going to write a persuasive text.**

## Should Every Child Play a Sport?

Some people think every child should play a sport. Other people do not agree.

**What do you think?**

Write to persuade a reader to agree with your opinion.

- Start with a clear opinion.
- Give reasons for your opinion and explain them.
- Finish with a strong conclusion.

Remember to: plan your writing • write in sentences • use paragraphs • check your spelling and punctuation.

# Paper 2 — Reading（40分钟，24题）

## Text A — 故事

**The Piano Lesson**

Marcus hated Tuesday afternoons. Tuesday meant piano lessons with Mrs Petrov, whose house smelled of lavender and whose metronome ticked like a bomb.

"Again," Mrs Petrov would say. "From the beginning." She said it so often that Marcus wondered if she knew any other words.

One Tuesday, Marcus arrived to find the front door open and boxes stacked in the hallway. Mrs Petrov was wrapping picture frames in newspaper.

"I am moving to be near my daughter," she said. "Today is our last lesson."

Marcus felt something strange in his stomach. He should have been happy — no more scales, no more ticking metronome! But instead, his throat felt tight.

"Play for me," said Mrs Petrov, closing the piano books. "Not the exercises. Play anything you like."

Marcus placed his hands on the keys and played the tune he had made up himself, the one he only played at home when nobody was listening. When he finished, the room was quiet.

"You see?" said Mrs Petrov softly. "All those scales, all that 'again, again' — it was for this. So your hands could play what your heart hears."

Marcus practised every day after that. And years later, when he played his first concert, he put a sprig of lavender on top of the piano.

**Q5.** What did Mrs Petrov ask Marcus to do in the last lesson?

**Q6.** What did Mrs Petrov mean by "So your hands could play what your heart hears"?

## Text B — 说明文（含步骤元素）

**How Bees Make Honey**

Honey does not come from a factory. It is made by one of nature's hardest workers: the honeybee.

*Step 1: Collecting nectar.* A worker bee flies from flower to flower, sucking up a sweet juice called nectar with her long tongue. She stores it in a special "honey stomach". To fill it, she may visit more than 1 000 flowers in one trip!

*Step 2: Passing it on.* Back at the hive, she passes the nectar to a "house bee", who chews it for about half an hour. Special chemicals in the bee's mouth begin to turn the nectar into honey.

*Step 3: Drying it out.* The house bees spread the nectar into the honeycomb cells. Then they fan it with their wings to dry out the water, making the honey thick and sticky.

*Step 4: Sealing the cell.* When the honey is ready, the bees cap the cell with wax to keep it clean — like putting a lid on a jar.

A single bee makes only about half a teaspoon of honey in her whole life. So next time you spread honey on your toast, remember how many tiny workers made your breakfast possible!

**Q9.** How many flowers might a bee visit in one trip?

**Q10.** What do house bees do to dry out the nectar?

**Q11.** The bees cap the cell with wax —

**Q12.** The text compares capping the cell to "putting a lid on a jar" to help the reader —

**Q13.** In what order does nectar become honey?
- A) fanned → chewed → collected → capped
- B) collected → chewed → fanned → capped
- C) capped → collected → chewed → fanned

**Q14.** The last paragraph is mainly there to —

## Text C — 诗歌

**Grandad's Shed**

Grandad's shed is a treasure chest,
though the roof is bent and grey.
Inside are jars of sleeping screws
and tools from yesterday.

A bicycle with one good wheel
leans dreaming on the wall,
and paint tins stacked like circus clowns
seem certain they will fall.

"One day I'll fix it," Grandad says,
of everything in sight.
But nothing here is broken —
to me, it's all just right.

**Q18.** The bicycle "leans dreaming on the wall". The poet makes the bicycle sound —
